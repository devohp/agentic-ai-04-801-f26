# Data

The curated capstone dataset for 04-801, Fall 2026. Built 2026-10-30; weather and soil retrieved 2026-10-27.

**Read this first.** The dataset is **synthetic and calibrated**. The fields, farms, enumerators, management records and NDVI values are invented for teaching. The weather and the soil values are real, and the survey-style numbers follow real survey distributions. Nothing here describes a real farm, and your system should never claim more than its inputs support (briefing, section 5.4).

| Layer | Status | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| Daily weather (`weather_daily.csv`) | **real, unmodified** | NASA POWER daily point data per site, exactly as the API returned it, including its `-999` fill values |
| Topsoil (`fields.csv`) | **real** | iSDAsoil predictions (0 to 20 cm) at each field's coordinates, with the 68 percent interval as delivered |
| Yields, fertilizer, labour, area (`field_seasons.csv`) | **synthetic, calibrated** | Generated by a model, then mapped onto the weighted EPAR distributions for Tanzania NPS Wave 5 (2020/21) |
| NDVI (`ndvi_monthly.csv`) | **synthetic, in the style of Digital Earth Africa** | Same columns and formula as the Monthly NDVI Anomaly product of Digital Earth Africa (DE Africa), but the values are modelled from crop phenology and the real rainfall; they are not DE Africa data |
| Field notes (`field_notes/`) | **synthetic** | Written for this course in the style of extension officers |
| Agronomy snippets (`snippets/`) | **paraphrased, sourced** | Short texts written for this course; each names its source. Some snippets have no source on purpose |

Like real field data, the package contains a small number of deliberately planted, realistic imperfections. There is no list of them. Finding and handling them is part of the work; say in `DECISIONS.md` what you found and what you did.

## Sites and seasons

All six sites grow maize in one main season per year. A season is labelled by its harvest year.

| `site_id` | Location | Season regime | Window | Calendar source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TZ-MBZ | Mbozi, Songwe, Tanzania | Msimu (unimodal) | Nov to Jun | FAO GIEWS (2026a) |
| TZ-IRG | Ismani, Iringa, Tanzania | Msimu (unimodal) | Nov to Jun | FAO GIEWS (2026a) |
| TZ-SNG | Songea rural, Ruvuma, Tanzania | Msimu (unimodal) | Nov to Jun | FAO GIEWS (2026a) |
| TZ-HAI | Hai lowlands, Kilimanjaro, Tanzania | Masika (bimodal, long rains) | Feb to Aug | FAO GIEWS (2026a) |
| KE-BMT | Rift Valley highlands, Kenya (the briefing's Kenya reference point) | Long rains, high-potential area | Feb to Sep, harvest from Oct | FAO GIEWS (2026b) |
| RW-WST | Western Province, Rwanda (the briefing's Rwanda reference point) | Season B | Feb to Jul | FAO GIEWS (2016) |

Seasons 2022 to 2025 carry yield labels. **Season 2026 is a holdout:** its rows have planting dates and inputs, but `harvest_date`, `harvest_kg` and `yield_kg_ha` are empty. Use it to test in-season detection; the labels stay with the teaching team.

## Column dictionary

**`sites.csv`**: `site_id`; `site_name`; `country` (TZ, KE, RW); `latitude`, `longitude` (decimal degrees, WGS 84; the point used for the weather request); `season_regime`; `season_window` (months).

**`fields.csv`** (soil source: iSDAsoil v2, depth 0 to 20 cm, retrieved 2026-10-27)

| Column | Unit | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `field_id` | | `SITE-Fnn` |
| `latitude`, `longitude` | decimal degrees | an invented field placement on real land, 0 to 5 km from the site point; at KE-BMT-F01 and RW-WST-F01 it is exactly the briefing's reference point |
| `ph`, `ph_ci68_low`, `ph_ci68_high` | pH units | prediction and 68 percent interval, unchanged |
| `carbon_organic_g_kg` (+ `_ci68_low`, `_ci68_high`) | g/kg | |
| `nitrogen_total_g_kg` (+ CI) | g/kg | |
| `phosphorous_extractable_ppm` (+ CI) | ppm (mg/kg) | iSDA spelling; the weakest layer, R squared 0.486 (iSDA, n.d.) |
| `sand_pct`, `clay_pct` | percent | |
| `crop_cover_2019_pct` | percent | iSDA crop cover layer for 2019; used to place the fields on cropland |

For the properties it offers, your live soil tool returns the same values at these coordinates, unless iSDA updates its model.

**`weather_daily.csv`** (NASA POWER, daily point API, community AG, time standard LST)

| Column | Unit |
|---|---|
| `site_id`, `date` | |
| `T2M`, `T2M_MAX`, `T2M_MIN` | degrees C, at 2 m |
| `PRECTOTCORR` | mm/day (bias-corrected precipitation) |
| `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN` | MJ/m2/day (all-sky surface shortwave) |
| `RH2M` | percent, at 2 m |
| `source` | the `sources` header POWER returned for that request (see below) |

`-999` means missing. Range 2021-10-01 to 2026-10-20. The data were requested one calendar year per site, and `source` carries the `sources` header POWER returned for that request, which lists every source used for any of the six parameters: `NASA POWER, MERRA2/SYN1DEG/POWER` for 2021 to 2025, and `NASA POWER, MERRA2/GEOSIT/SYN1DEG/FLASHFLUX/POWER` for 2026, whose recent days include provisional near-real-time values (GEOSIT for the meteorology) that POWER revises later. The course weather tools request only `T2M` and `PRECTOTCORR`, so their label is shorter. This file is frozen as of 2026-10-27. For the most recent weeks it will not match a live call, and it is not supposed to.

**`ndvi_monthly.csv`** (synthetic, in the style of DE Africa)

| Column | Note |
|---|---|
| `field_id`, `month` | month as `YYYY-MM`, 2021-10 to 2026-09 |
| `ndvi_mean` | mean NDVI of the month; empty if there was no clear observation |
| `ndvi_clim_mean`, `ndvi_clim_std` | climatology for that field and calendar month; here computed over the synthetic seasons in this file, not over DE Africa's 1984 to 2020 baseline |
| `ndvi_std_anomaly` | `(ndvi_mean - ndvi_clim_mean) / ndvi_clim_std` |
| `clear_count` | number of clear observations in the month |

The column names and the anomaly formula follow the Monthly NDVI Anomaly product of DE Africa (`ndvi_mean`, `ndvi_std_anomaly`, `clear_count`; Digital Earth Africa, n.d.): `ndvi_std_anomaly` = (`ndvi_mean` - `ndvi_clim_mean`) / `ndvi_clim_std`, where the climatology is the mean and standard deviation of that field's NDVI in the same calendar month across the seasons in this file (October 2021 to September 2026). Your own baseline, for example a golden-season profile, will give different anomalies; that is the point of the stretch goal in Challenge A. The values come from a crop-growth curve per field and season, scaled by the field's outcome and damped by water stress computed from the real rainfall, plus noise. They are plausible, not observed. A team that wants real satellite data may use the DE Africa Sandbox as a stretch goal.

## Calibration

Weighted, winsorized EPAR indicators for Tanzania NPS Wave 5, 2020/21 (EPAR, 2026; commit `ad6d7de`):

| Quantity | p25 | Median | p75 | EPAR indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maize yield, kg/ha | 408.7 | 807.1 | 1,421.5 | `w_yield_hv_maize` |
| Fertilizer rate among users, kg/ha | 8.8 | 30.9 | 83.5 | `w_inorg_fert_rate_allusers` |
| Family labour, person-days | 15 | 55 | 132 | `w_labor_family` |
| Hired labour, person-days | 0 | 0 | 5 | `w_labor_hired` |
| Planted maize area, ha | 0.25 | 0.53 | 1.13 | `w_total_planted_area_maize` |

Share of households using inorganic fertilizer: 0.16 (`use_inorg_fert`). In this dataset: labelled maize yields (2022 to 2025, as generated, before any recording problems) have quartiles 410 / 807 / 1,417 kg/ha, and inorganic fertilizer is used in 16% of field-seasons.

Two simplifications you should name in your report. First, the Tanzanian distributions are applied to all six sites, so the Kenyan and Rwandan fields inherit Tanzanian yield ranges and the Tanzanian fertilizer adoption rate; differences between countries in this dataset are not evidence about the countries. Second, household-level labour and area indicators are applied to single fields.
